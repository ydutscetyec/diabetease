HOW THIS WORKS

User guide

How to use DiabetEASE, understand your results, and find care.

What this is

A research prototype that helps you understand Type 1 diabetes risk, find local testing centres, and learn about the condition. It does not replace a doctor and does not diagnose.

1. The risk assessment

Five steps, about two minutes: about you, family history, symptoms, laboratory results, and a review before you submit.

Leave any field blank if you do not know the answer. Blanks are treated as unknown, never as normal.

2. Understanding your result

There are five outcomes:

  • Emergency Seek emergency care now. Nothing else on the page matters.
  • See a doctor promptly A value met a published diagnostic threshold, or two or more islet autoantibodies were reported.
  • Worth getting tested Nothing met a threshold, but there is enough to justify a glucose test.
  • Not enough information No laboratory value was given, so no assessment is possible.
  • Nothing crosses a threshold Reassuring about today. Not an all-clear — early-stage Type 1 diabetes is silent on a glucose test.

3. Finding a testing centre

Open the Testing centre map. Pin colours: teal a confirmed service, orange not yet confirmed, red record outdated. Pharmacies are square teal markers.

Filter by municipality, by confirmed service, or switch between testing centres and pharmacies. Call before travelling: most records are still unconfirmed.

4. Chatting with Candie

Open Candie from the round button in the corner. She can explain Type 1 diabetes, say what a test measures in general, walk through a result you recorded this session, and look up testing centres by municipality.

She cannot diagnose you, interpret your own results, or advise on medication or insulin doses. She will say so rather than guess.

5. Your privacy

No name, birth date, address or contact number is collected anywhere. Assessments are discarded after the result is computed. If an operator enables storage and you consent, the record holds a banded age and summary counts, never an exact age, and carries a deletion date (90 days by default).

The design follows data minimisation under the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173). A privacy notice alone does not make a system compliant; the controls have to exist and be audited.

6. What this system cannot do

  • No risk percentage or score. No model has been trained, so none is produced.
  • No forecast of future onset. That needs data following people over time.
  • No confirmation that a listed facility offers a test, unless the record says confirmed with a source and date.
  • No laboratory testing. Values must come from a laboratory you have already visited.

LEARN AT YOUR OWN PACE

Educational resources

Type 1 diabetes, testing and when to seek care.

When to go to an emergency department

Vomiting, stomach pain, deep or rapid breathing, fruity-smelling breath, drowsiness or confusion — especially alongside heavy thirst and frequent urination — can mean diabetic ketoacidosis. Go now. Do not wait for an appointment.

What it is

The immune system destroys the beta cells in the pancreas that make insulin. Without insulin the body cannot move glucose out of the blood and into cells, so blood glucose climbs while the body starves.

It is not caused by eating sugar. Nothing a child or parent did caused it, and no diet or exercise plan prevents it.

How it develops

The autoimmune process starts months to years before any symptom appears. Stage 1 is two or more islet autoantibodies with normal glucose; stage 2 adds rising glucose without symptoms; stage 3 is glucose that meets the diagnostic criteria, with or without symptoms.

This is why a check like this one cannot rule the condition out — early stages are silent.

Family history and genes

About 85 in 100 people who develop Type 1 diabetes have no family history. A parent or sibling with it raises the risk to roughly 1 in 20, against about 1 in 300 generally. Those figures are from studies in the United States; none exists for the Philippines.

Islet autoantibodies

GAD65, IA-2, ZnT8 and insulin autoantibodies are the markers used to identify presymptomatic Type 1 diabetes. Two or more persistently positive antibodies defines early-stage disease and needs a clinician to confirm and stage.

Glucose and HbA1c tests

Fasting glucose, random glucose, the oral glucose tolerance test and HbA1c are the standard tests. They show whether glucose is abnormal — they do not say which type of diabetes it is. Telling Type 1 from Type 2 needs antibody testing and clinical judgement.

Getting tested through PhilHealth

PhilHealth’s primary care package, YAKAP (called Konsulta before 2025), lists fasting blood sugar, the glucose tolerance test and HbA1c among its covered laboratory tests when the doctor at your registered provider orders them.

If that clinic has no laboratory, the test may be done at a partner laboratory instead. Your municipal health office can tell you how to register.

Risk check, screening, diagnosis

A risk check sorts people by who might benefit from testing. Screening is an actual laboratory test offered to people without symptoms. Diagnosis is a clinician confirming the condition. DiabetEASE only does the first.

Set up open conversation

Candie answers reviewed topics on her own. To let her answer anything else, run a language model on this computer. It is detected automatically.

  1. Install Ollama from ollama.com.
  2. In PowerShell: ollama pull llama3.2
  3. Leave Ollama running and reload this page.

If answers are slow or time out, a smaller model usually fixes it: ollama pull llama3.2:1b

Privacy

Collected: age, sex, municipality, family history, symptoms and any laboratory values entered. No name, birth date, address, phone number or email is requested.

Stored: nothing by default. When storage is enabled and consented to, records hold a banded age and summary counts with a scheduled deletion date.

Transmitted: answers are sent to the DiabetEASE server only. No third-party service receives them.

Legal basis: data minimisation under Republic Act No. 10173.
